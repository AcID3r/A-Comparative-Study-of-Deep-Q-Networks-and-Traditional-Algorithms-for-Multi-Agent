A Comparative Study of Deep Q-Networks and Traditional Algorithms for Multi-
Agent Shortest-Path Search in Dynamic Graphs
Madhav Joshi
Marwadi University
Abstract—This study compares prioritized breadth-first search, Dijkstra and A* with solo-trained and shared-
policy deep Q-networks for navigation on static and changing unit-cost grids. Ten policies from five training seeds
were frozen after static 5×5 training and evaluated without learning on 120 new geometries spanning 5×5, 10×10
and 15×15 sizes, one/two/four agents and two wall probabilities. The final protocol produced 15,120 unique
evaluation episodes; three fresh timing rollouts per episode were not treated as extra outcome samples. Across all
conditions, replanning A* completed 642/720 episodes (89.17%), shared DQN 799/3600 (22.19%), and solo DQN
870/3600 (24.17%). All recorded first trajectories passed replay auditing, and actual collisions were zero under the
common rejection rules. Success-conditioned paths, matched comparisons and descriptive variation across model
seeds are reported alongside failures. The findings apply to fixed small-map-trained policies and current-map
prioritized planners under this protocol; they do not establish a universal algorithm ranking or a general
reinforcement-learning adaptation advantage.
Index Terms—graph search; deep Q-network; multi-agent pathfinding; dynamic grid; policy transfer.
